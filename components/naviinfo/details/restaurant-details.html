Our Story

A Legacy of Flavor

From Garden to Table, Since 2009

Aurelia & Vine was born from a simple dream: to create a dining experience where every ingredient is honored, every flavor is intentional, and every guest feels like family. Founded by Chef Elara Moreau in the autumn of 2009, what began as a humble twelve-seat bistro has blossomed into one of the most celebrated dining destinations in the country.

Our philosophy is rooted in the farm-to-table movement. We partner with over thirty local farms, fisheries, and vineyards to source the freshest seasonal ingredients. Our rooftop herb garden supplies the kitchen daily with basil, rosemary, thyme, and edible flowers that garnish your plate.

Chef Moreau's diverse culinary background — trained in Lyon, refined in Tokyo, and inspired by her Mediterranean roots — informs a menu that defies categorization. Each dish is a harmonious blend of techniques and traditions from around the world.

— Chef Elara Moreau, Founder & Executive Chef

Signature Recipes

Recreate a taste of Aurelia & Vine at home with these exclusive recipes from Chef Moreau's personal collection.

01

Golden Saffron Shrimp

25 min • Serves 4 • Intermediate

Ingredients

  • 12 large tiger shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 1 tsp saffron threads, steeped in 2 tbsp warm water
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 ripe avocado, whipped smooth
  • Zest and juice of 1 Meyer lemon
  • 1 tbsp citrus caviar (or substitute with small lemon segments)
  • Micro-greens and edible flowers for garnish
  • Sea salt and white pepper to taste

Preparation

  1. Steep saffron threads in warm water for 10 minutes until the liquid turns a deep gold.
  2. Pat shrimp dry and season with salt and white pepper. Heat olive oil in a cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat.
  3. Sear shrimp for 90 seconds per side until golden. In the last 30 seconds, add the saffron infusion and lemon juice. Toss to coat.
  4. Spread a thin layer of whipped avocado across each plate. Arrange three shrimp per plate on top.
  5. Spoon the saffron pan sauce over the shrimp. Garnish with citrus caviar, micro-greens, and edible flowers.
  6. Serve immediately while the shrimp are warm and the avocado is cool for a beautiful temperature contrast.
Chef's Note: The key to this dish is the contrast — warm shrimp against cool avocado, bold saffron against delicate citrus. Don't overcook the shrimp; they should be just opaque. — Chef Moreau

Emerald Herb Crusted Lamb

1 hr 30 min • Serves 6 • Advanced

Ingredients

  • 2 racks of lamb (8 ribs each), frenched
  • 1 cup pistachios, finely ground
  • 1/2 cup fresh parsley, minced
  • 1/4 cup fresh rosemary, minced
  • 3 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 2 cups panko breadcrumbs
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 large parsnip, peeled and cubed
  • 2 cups beef stock, reduced to 1 cup
  • 2 tbsp butter, cold and cubed
  • Salt and cracked black pepper

Preparation

  1. Preheat oven to 200°C (400°F). Season lamb racks generously with salt and pepper.
  2. Sear lamb fat-side down in a hot skillet for 3 minutes per side until deeply browned. Let rest 10 minutes.
  3. Brush the fat side of each rack with Dijon mustard. Combine pistachios, parsley, rosemary, panko, and olive oil. Press the mixture firmly onto the mustard-coated side.
  4. Roast for 22–25 minutes for medium-rare (internal temperature 57°C / 135°F). Rest for 10 minutes before carving.
  5. Meanwhile, boil parsnip until tender. Blend with a splash of cream and butter until silky smooth. Season well.
  6. Warm the reduced beef stock, whisk in cold butter cubes for a glossy jus. Plate parsnip puree, slice lamb into double chops, and drizzle with rosemary jus.
Chef's Note: Use a meat thermometer — lamb is unforgiving. The crust should be emerald-green and nutty; if it browns too fast, tent with foil. — Chef Moreau

Gold Dust Soufflé

45 min • Serves 4 • Intermediate

Ingredients

  • 200g dark chocolate (70% cacao), chopped
  • 4 large eggs, separated
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar (plus extra for ramekins)
  • 1 tbsp butter (for greasing ramekins)
  • 1 tsp vanilla bean paste
  • 1/4 tsp cream of tartar
  • 8 fresh strawberries, sliced
  • Edible gold dust for garnish
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream, softly whipped

Preparation

  1. Preheat oven to 190°C (375°F). Butter four ramekins thoroughly and coat with sugar, tapping out excess.
  2. Melt chocolate in a double boiler over gently simmering water. Stir until smooth, then remove from heat and let cool slightly.
  3. Whisk egg yolks with 1/4 cup sugar and vanilla until pale and ribbony. Fold into the melted chocolate.
  4. In a clean bowl, beat egg whites with cream of tartar until soft peaks form. Gradually add remaining sugar and beat to stiff, glossy peaks.
  5. Fold one-third of the whites into the chocolate to lighten, then gently fold in the remainder. Divide among ramekins, filling to the top.
  6. Bake for 14–16 minutes until risen and set on the edges but still jiggly in the center. Dust with gold, add strawberries and cream. Serve immediately — soufflés wait for no one.
Chef's Note: The most common mistake is opening the oven door. Don't. Trust the timer. The soufflé should rise dramatically and have a slight wobble when gently shaken. — Chef Moreau
